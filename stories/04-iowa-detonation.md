# Story 4 - The Iowa detonation (Around the World 1)

**Headline as printed:** US army to detonate 68 barrels of
toxic lead in Iowa on day before Thanksgiving

**Position:** Around the World, row 1, left cell. The source of
the numbers strip's lead figure.

**Carriers:** AP News, The Guardian (two).

**Region:** United States.

**Panel label:** IOWA.

## What happened

The US Army is set to detonate 68 barrels, five tons, of lead
azide at an ammunition plant in rural Iowa on Wednesday
morning, the day before the Thanksgiving holiday. Residents
were warned to stay indoors amid conflicting information about
how far the dust plume could travel. Lead azide is a primary
explosive that grows more dangerous as it degrades, which is
why the material is being destroyed where it sits rather than
moved.

## The facts on record

- The stockpile is 68 barrels, five tons, of lead azide.
- The detonation is set for Wednesday morning, the day before
  Thanksgiving, at an Iowa ammunition plant.
- Residents were warned to stay indoors.
- Officials gave conflicting accounts of the plume's reach.
- The blast was scheduled for 10am ET on Wednesday.

## The carriers, weighed

AP is the wire of record for the plan itself (barrels, tonnage,
timing), and the Guardian's contribution is the human perimeter:
the resident warning and the conflicting plume accounts, in
fuller form than the wire carried. The page's facts combine the
two without blending their authority: figures are AP's, the
warning is the Guardian's, and the conflict is both of theirs,
which is why it could be printed as a conflict rather than as
one side's claim.

## Why it leads the grid

It carries the day's most concrete number and the day's most
practical warning at once. The features were full (a killing, a
conviction, a verdict), and this story's weight is a fact and a
figure rather than a decision, which is the grid's definition.
It also anchors the page's American story, which the explainer
beside it then serves.

## How the headline was chosen

The headline keeps the barrel count because the count is the
story's scale in one word, and keeps the Thanksgiving timing
because the timing is why the warning mattered that week: a
holiday population at home, indoors, is the population the
warning was written for.

## What was not printed

A single plume distance: the officials' accounts conflicted,
and printing either figure as fact would have chosen a side in
an unresolved safety question. The page prints the conflict
instead, in the facts, which is the honest version.

## The headline, phrase by phrase

"US army to detonate" is future tense and stays future: at
press time the blast had not happened, and the page does not
report plans as events. "68 barrels" is the scale in the
carriers' own unit; the tonnage (five) is in the facts, because
a reader pictures barrels at a plant gate more easily than
tons. "Toxic lead" is the hazard in two words. "On day before
Thanksgiving" is the timing, kept because the warning's whole
difficulty was reaching a holiday population.
