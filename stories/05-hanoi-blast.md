# Story 5 - The Hanoi blast (Around the World 2)

**Headline as printed:** A blast at a hot pot eatery in
Vietnam's capital Hanoi kills 6 and injures more than a dozen

**Position:** Around the World, row 1, right cell. One of the
edition's two panel stories: the wire copy carried no
photograph, so the slot is a navy panel labelled VIETNAM.

**Carriers:** AP News, Reuters (two wires, no shared ownership).

**Region:** Southeast Asia.

**Panel label:** VIETNAM (drawn, because there is no photograph).

## What happened

A blast at a hot pot eatery in Hanoi killed six people and
injured more than a dozen others, news agencies reported from
the scene. It was the deadliest single incident reported from
Vietnam in the day's intake. Hot pot restaurants cook at the
table over open burners, which is the context the record notes
and the context a reader needs before asking how an eatery
blast kills six people.

## The facts on record

- Six people were killed.
- More than a dozen were injured.
- The blast was at a hot pot eatery in Hanoi.
- Two wires carried it from the scene.

## Why it is in the grid

It is the day's deadliest event by toll, and it is a wire
story: complete in its headline, with no decision pending and
no reversal inside it. Grid stories are facts at weight; a
six-death blast in a capital city is that, and it leads its row
with the Iowa story because both are events, not processes.

## How the headline was chosen

The headline is the wires' own full sentence, kept whole
because it carries toll, place and mechanism in one line. The
strip takes its second figure from this story, with the label
repeating the attribution to the story rather than to a body,
because the number is a wire count from a scene, not a report's
finding.

## What was not printed

A cause: the wires reported the blast, not its origin, and the
page does not speculate about gas, equipment or anything else.
The eatery's name and district: not in both wires' copy.

## Watch next

The injured count resolving, and the first official statement
on cause, which converts this from a toll story into an
accountability story.

## The headline, phrase by phrase

"A blast" is the wires' noun and the page keeps its
indefiniteness: the copy establishes an explosion, not its
mechanism. "At a hot pot eatery" is the setting that makes the
toll intelligible. "In Vietnam's capital Hanoi" names city and
country both, because the grid's readers may know either and
the panel beside the headline says only VIETNAM. "Kills 6 and
injures more than a dozen" is the toll in the wires' own
precision: an exact count for the dead, an honest vagueness
for the injured, whose number was still moving.
