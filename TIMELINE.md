# The day in brief — Wednesday 25 November 2026

A chronology of the day this edition covers, assembled from the
confirming outlets' reports. Times are as the outlets gave them;
where a report gave no time, the event is placed by the part of
the day the reporting describes. This page exists so a reader can
reconstruct the order in which the day's news actually happened,
which the front page, organised by importance, deliberately does
not show.

## Tuesday, the day before

- Israeli strikes in Gaza kill seven Palestinians, among them
  Naeem Abu Nahel, Hamas's internal security chief, two months
  into the post. The strikes are reported on Tuesday; the news
  leads Wednesday's papers because confirmation and reaction
  arrived overnight.
- A New York jury reaches its verdict in the Huawei trial after
  months of proceedings: guilty of conspiracy to steal trade
  secrets, not guilty of racketeering. The verdict lands on
  Tuesday American time and is Wednesday's business lead in
  Europe and Asia.
- In South Africa, a court convicts a Zimbabwean man of trying
  to travel to fight for Russia in Ukraine, a case built on the
  recruitment pipeline Ukraine says has drawn in hundreds of
  Africans.

## Wednesday morning

- The US Army's planned detonation at the Iowa ammunition plant
  dominates American local coverage: 68 barrels, five tons, of
  degraded lead azide are to be blown up at 10am Eastern, with
  residents warned to stay indoors and officials disputing how
  far the lead dust plume might travel.
- Wire explainers on the Iowa plan move alongside the spot news,
  answering why degraded lead azide cannot simply be trucked
  away and why open detonation is the standard remedy.
- In Hanoi, a blast at a hot pot restaurant kills six people and
  injures more than a dozen; both AP and Reuters move the story
  during the Asian day.

## Wednesday, through the day

- Published extracts from the private diary of the late Pope
  Francis circulate, recording his belief that he saw the devil
  three times, once in the choir loft during Mass. BBC News and
  PBS both carry the story, which becomes the day's most shared
  item despite, or because of, fitting no ordinary category.
- Reaction to the Gaza strike and the Huawei verdict continues
  through the day without changing the confirmed facts either
  story rests on; the edition prints the confirmed core of each
  and none of the day's speculation around them.

## What the order shows

Read in time order, the day has no single thread: a war strike,
two courtrooms on two continents, a planned explosion, a
restaurant disaster and a dead pope's diary. That is precisely
why the front page is organised by consequence instead of by
the clock. The chronology is kept here, in the record, and the
page is kept for the ranking.

## Reading the order

Chronology runs: the diary's publication, the verdict at the
end of its months-long trial, the conviction, the strike
reported from Tuesday, the blast, and the detonation still to
come. The page runs almost none of that order. It leads with
the strike because consequence outranks sequence, places the
two court outcomes beside it because decisions belong
together, and puts the future event (Iowa) at the head of the
grid because it is the only story whose main fact had not
happened yet. A reader who wants the clock can rebuild it from
this file; a reader who wants the day reads the page. Both
readers are reading the same record, which is the point of
keeping the two orders in two documents.

## The hours the record cannot give

The feeds timestamp publication, not events, and this edition's
events resist clock times more than most: a strike reported on
Tuesday's cycle, a trial that ran for months and ended in a
verdict, a detonation still in the future at press time, a
diary written across years and published in a day. The timeline
therefore orders by the day the record supports and declines to
invent hours. A front page is allowed to rank; a timeline is
not allowed to fabricate, and the difference between the two
documents is exactly that permission.
