# Gazetteer — the places on this page

Short notes on every place named on the front page, in the order
the stories appear. A one-image paper cannot carry maps; this
document is where the geography lives instead.

## Gaza

The Gaza Strip is the smaller of the two Palestinian territories,
a coastal enclave between Israel, Egypt and the Mediterranean.
Its internal security forces answer to Hamas, which has governed
the territory since 2007. The strike in this edition's lead story
killed the man who had run those forces for the past two months.

## South Africa and Ukraine

The conviction happened in South Africa, whose courts have
jurisdiction over its residents' and visitors' attempts to join
foreign wars without authorisation. The war the convicted man
tried to reach is Russia's invasion of Ukraine, now in its fifth
year, whose manpower demands have drawn recruits from far beyond
either belligerent, including, Kyiv says, hundreds of Africans.

## New York

The Huawei trial sat in New York, in the federal court for the
Eastern District of New York, where the original indictments
were filed. American federal courtrooms do not allow cameras,
which is why the page's third feature image is a courtroom
sketch rather than a photograph, and why both confirming outlets
illustrated the verdict the same way.

## Iowa

The Iowa Army Ammunition Plant, near Middletown in the state's
southeast, has made and stored munitions since the Second World
War. The stockpile destroyed in this edition's grid stories is
lead azide, the primary explosive used in detonators, degraded
with age into a state too sensitive to transport. The plant sits
in rural country, which is why a controlled open detonation,
with residents warned rather than evacuated, was the chosen
method.

## Hanoi

Hanoi is Vietnam's capital, in the country's north. The blast in
this edition happened at a hot pot restaurant, where diners cook
food at the table over portable burners. Six deaths made it the
deadliest single incident in the day's intake from Asia, and it
is one of the two stories on the page without a photograph.

## The Vatican

Pope Francis, who died in 2025, was the first Latin American
pope and the first Jesuit to hold the office. The diary extracts
published this week were written in his own hand during his
pontificate. The choir loft he mentions is the raised gallery in
a church where the choir sings during Mass, in his case inside
the Vatican's own chapels.

## Why a gazetteer in a code repository

The competition entry is a program, but the program's product is
journalism. The places above are the difference between seven
headlines and a day in the world; keeping their notes beside the
code keeps the entry honest about what it is summarising.

## Three more places the record touches

- **The choir loft** is a place in this edition: the setting
  Francis' diary gives for one of the three visions. Small
  places get entries when a story's most checkable detail is
  located in them; a reader can find the loft in any large
  church, which makes the detail testable in a way the vision
  itself is not.
- **New York** appears as a courtroom before it appears as a
  city: the Huawei trial's venue is part of the verdict's
  weight, because the prosecution was an American one and the
  jury an American jury.
- **The ammunition plant** in rural Iowa is the edition's
  second institutional place: a site whose stockpile a whole
  community lives beside, and the reason a disposal plan is
  front-page news there and a wire brief everywhere else.
