# Hot pot restaurants and table-top burners

Background for grid story 2 (the Hanoi blast). The dossier is
stories/05-hanoi-blast.md.

## Why an eatery can produce a blast

Hot pot is cooked at the table: a simmering broth over a live
burner, often a portable gas burner with a small canister,
surrounded by diners. The format puts fuel, flame and people in
the closest possible arrangement, which is why the context
record notes the cooking method before anything else: it is
the fact a reader needs to understand how a restaurant blast
kills six people and injures more than a dozen.

## What the page does not claim

The wires reported a blast, a toll and injuries. They did not,
in the copy the page was built from, establish a cause. This
briefing therefore explains the setting and stops there: no
canister, leak or appliance is named as the cause anywhere in
this edition, because naming one would be invention. The
distinction between explaining how such a blast is possible
and stating what caused this one is the whole of the desk's
discipline on accident stories.

## Toll discipline

Six killed and more than a dozen injured are wire figures from
a scene, printed as the strip's second number with the story as
their source. Scene counts move, usually up in the first day as
the injured are accounted for. The dossier's watch note says
what the desk is waiting for: the count resolving and a first
official statement on cause.

## The panel

This story is one of the edition's two without a photograph.
Its slot is a navy panel labelled VIETNAM. A stock image of a
hot pot restaurant would have illustrated the cuisine and lied
about the event; the panel illustrates nothing and lies about
nothing, which is the correct trade.

## Wire tolls and their grammar

Wire copy from a blast scene has a grammar this edition follows
exactly: the dead are counted in figures ("kills 6"), the
injured in phrases ("more than a dozen"), because the dead at a
scene can be counted and the injured, scattered across
hospitals and still arriving, cannot yet. A reader who learns
the grammar can read any wire toll in the paper and know which
number is settled and which is still moving. The strip takes
the settled one, as it always does, and the dossier's watch
note waits for the other to settle.

## Hanoi, briefly

The blast happened in a capital city at a meal, which is why
the story travelled: capitals concentrate wire bureaus, and a
lunchtime or evening blast in an eatery is the kind of event
every bureau can reach within the hour. That reach explains the
confirmation's strength (two wires, from the scene) and its
thinness (nothing beyond the scene yet) in the same breath, and
the dossier records both halves of that explanation.

## Eating out, and why the setting is ordinary

Hot pot eateries are neighbourhood institutions across Vietnam:
communal, inexpensive, built around the table-top burner this
briefing describes. The ordinariness matters to the record
because it is what the toll interrupts. Six people were not at
a hazardous site by any ordinary understanding; they were at
dinner. Accident reporting that loses the ordinariness of the
setting loses the reason the story is news, and the desk's
context paragraph keeps the burners and the broth in view for
exactly that reason.
