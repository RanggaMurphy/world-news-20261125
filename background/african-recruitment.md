# African recruitment into Russia's war

Background for feature 2 (the Zimbabwean conviction). The
dossier is stories/02-zimbabwean-conviction.md.

## The pattern

Ukraine says hundreds of people from different African
countries have been recruited to fight for Russia. The reported
routes vary: work and study offers that become contracts,
recruiters operating through social media, and men who say they
did not know what they had signed. Kyiv has raised the
recruitment repeatedly this year, which is why a single
conviction in a South African court is a feature and not a
brief: it is a court, on the record, confirming one strand of
the pattern.

## Why South Africa is the venue

South Africa has laws against its residents and visitors
joining foreign military ventures, and a Zimbabwean national
passing through its jurisdiction gave its courts the case. The
conviction's importance is jurisdictional as much as moral: it
shows the recruitment pipeline passes through states whose
courts can reach it, and that at least one of those states is
willing to prosecute.

## The desk's naming rule

The convicted man is described by nationality and role, not
named in the headline. He is one recruit in a pipeline; the
pipeline is the story. Principals whose office is the story
(a security chief, a pope) are named. Recruits are not made
famous by the page for the worst decision of their lives.

## What would change the story

A firmer number than "hundreds" from a source that counts, a
second jurisdiction prosecuting, or a recruiting network named
in court papers rather than in allegations.
