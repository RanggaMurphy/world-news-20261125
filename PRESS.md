# House style - The World Today

The standing rules of the paper, stated once per edition so the
edition's own documents can point here instead of repeating
them. The 25 November 2026 edition follows every rule below.

## Selection

1. Two outlets or the story does not run. One outlet, however
   good, is a rumour with a logo.
2. Brands of one owner count as the feeds list them; a story
   whose only confirmations share an owner is disclosed as such
   wherever the edition's record discusses confirmation.
3. Features are for consequence: a decision (a verdict, a
   conviction), an open toll, a reversal. The grid is for
   weight: facts the day cannot be understood without.
4. Explainers and analysis may run when two outlets carry them;
   their headlines must signal their genre, and the record must
   say what they are.
5. Selection is never balanced by hand. If the rule produces a
   page heavy in one region or one subject, the page is heavy,
   and the record says why.

## Headlines

6. The carriers' wording in substance; shortened only to fit,
   and the shortening recorded in the story's dossier.
7. Split outcomes print whole. A verdict, ruling or vote with
   two halves gets both halves in the headline or the story
   does not run as a feature.
8. Claims keep their status words. A diary's claim is a claim,
   a report's finding is a finding, and the headline carries
   the word that says so.
9. No number the carriers did not print. No rounding, ever.

## Numbers

10. The strip takes its figures from the stories on the page,
    each labelled with its story, never from the paper's own
    process counts alone.
11. Tolls print as they stand at press time: killed, injured,
    missing, separately, in the carriers' order.
12. Where officials conflict on a safety figure, the page
    prints the conflict, not a choice between the figures.

## Pictures and panels

13. The lead image of a carrying article is the thumbnail,
    cover-cropped into its slot. No letterbox, no stretch, no
    invented image.
14. A story without a photograph gets a navy panel with its
    place name. The panel is a design element, labelled as
    drawn in the record, never dressed as a picture.
15. No text is set on a photograph.

## Names and places

16. Private individuals in pipelines (recruits, defendants in
    recruitment cases) are described by role and nationality in
    headlines; principals whose office is the story are named.
17. Places are named at the precision two outlets support.
18. Organisations' and diarists' phrases keep their attribution
    attached everywhere they appear, including the strip.

## The page

19. No body text. Masthead, a date line at near-title scale,
    headlines, thumbnails or panels, numbers, sources.
20. The same event may appear twice when the day produced two
    distinct pieces about it and each passes rule 1; the
    duplication is disclosed in the record, never hidden.
21. Corrections belong to the next edition's record. A
    submitted image is never re-rendered except to prove
    determinism, which by construction changes nothing.

## The rules at work in this edition

Worked examples, so a future editor can see the rules deciding
real cases rather than floating above them:

- **Rule 1** decided the page's boundary: every story here has
  at least two of the seven tracked outlets behind it, and
  stories that led single-outlet feeds that day are absent,
  however strong they looked.
- **Rule 2** is why the Gaza strike's confirmation is described
  as a wire plus a house: two of its three carriers share an
  owner, and the record says so instead of counting to three
  and staying quiet.
- **Rule 3** put a verdict, a conviction and a strike in the
  features: a decision, a decision, and the day's gravest
  consequence.
- **Rule 4** admitted the Iowa explainer: two wires carried it,
  and its headline's question form signals its genre on the
  page itself.
- **Rule 5** is why the page is heavy with Iowa: the rule
  produced two Iowa stories, and nobody balanced one away.
- **Rule 6** is visible in the Huawei headline, which is long
  because the carriers' wording is long and the fit allowed it.
- **Rule 7** is the same headline's other half: the acquittal
  prints, whole, or the story would not have featured.
- **Rule 8** is the word "claims" in the diary headline and the
  words "diary says" in the strip's third label.
- **Rule 9** is why the strip reads 68, 6, 3 and not "about
  seventy, several, a few": the carriers printed figures, and
  the page prints figures.
- **Rule 11** is why Hanoi's injured are "more than a dozen"
  everywhere in this repository: the wires had not settled the
  count, so neither does the paper.
- **Rule 12** is the plume: two official distances, one printed
  conflict.
- **Rule 14** is the two navy panels, VIETNAM and IOWA, drawn
  and labelled as drawn.
- **Rule 16** is why the Zimbabwean defendant is a nationality
  and a role in the headline while Abu Nahel is a name in the
  facts: the office-holder's identity is the story; the
  recruit's is not.
- **Rule 20** is the Iowa duplication, disclosed in the README,
  the companion, the dossier and here.

A closing note on the examples: they are drawn from this
edition because a style guide's examples should be checkable
against the paper they describe. A future edition applying the
same rules to a different day will produce different examples,
and its PRESS.md should carry its own, replacing these rather
than inheriting them. Rules travel; examples stay with their
day.
