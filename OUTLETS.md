# The outlets behind this edition

The World Today prints a story only when at least two of the
outlets below carried it independently. This document records
what each outlet is, why it is on the master list, and what it
confirmed for the 25 November 2026 edition. The list is short on
purpose: a story that only the long tail of aggregators carried
does not reach the page, however dramatic it sounds.

## BBC and BBC News

The BBC is Britain's public broadcaster; its news site and its
BBC News wire-style feed are listed separately in the intake
because they sometimes publish the same report in different
editions, and a match between them alone is not treated as two
independent confirmations for selection purposes unless a third
outlet also carried the story. In this edition the BBC confirmed
the Gaza strike, the South Africa conviction, the Huawei verdict
and the Pope's diary story, and supplied three of the five
photographs the page uses, including the courtroom image for the
Huawei verdict and the portrait of Pope Francis.

## Reuters

Reuters is a wire agency: its reports are written to be republished
by other newsrooms, which makes it the backbone of cross-checking.
A Reuters confirmation means the story passed a wire desk whose
business is accuracy for republication, not a single paper's
audience. In this edition Reuters confirmed the Gaza strike, the
Hanoi blast and the Iowa explainer. Two of those arrived through
Google News carriage of the wire rather than a direct feed, which
the research record in SOURCES.md notes where it applies.

## AP News

The Associated Press is the American wire cooperative, owned by
its member newsrooms. Its value to this paper is the same as
Reuters' with a different centre of gravity: where Reuters is
strongest on world and markets, AP is strongest on United States
domestic reporting, which is why both Iowa stories rest on AP
confirmation. AP also co-confirmed the Hanoi blast, giving that
story the two-wire backing (AP and Reuters) that the selection
rule treats as the strongest form of confirmation.

## Al Jazeera

Al Jazeera is the Qatari international broadcaster. It is on the
master list because its reporting from the Middle East and Africa
frequently reaches stories the wires move later, and because its
framing differs enough from the BBC's that agreement between the
two is meaningful evidence. In this edition Al Jazeera confirmed
the South Africa conviction (with the BBC) and the Huawei verdict
(with BBC News), and its own report of the conviction is one of
the two links kept for that story in sources.json.

## The Guardian

The Guardian is a British national newspaper with a large
American operation. It is used here as a confirming outlet rather
than a wire: when a Guardian report and an AP report agree on
specifics, as they did on the Iowa detonation (68 barrels, five
tons, the 10am ET timing, the stay-indoors warning), the numbers
can be printed with confidence. The Guardian's photograph of the
Iowa Army Ammunition Plant entrance is the grid's first image.

## PBS

PBS is the American public broadcaster. It appears on the master
list for stories where American public media does original
reporting of its own, and in this edition it is the second
confirmation, alongside BBC News, for the Pope Francis diary
story. A story confirmed by a British and an American public
broadcaster, neither with a commercial reason to inflate it, is
exactly the kind of odd item the two-outlet rule exists to
admit: unusual, but solid.

## How confirmations were counted

- Two outlets count as independent when neither is republishing
  the other's copy. Wire copy carried by a newspaper under the
  wire's own byline counts as the wire, not the newspaper.
- The BBC's site and feed editions count as one outlet for the
  two-outlet rule; the Gaza story clears the bar through Reuters,
  not through the BBC appearing twice.
- Google News links in the research record are carriage links:
  they show where a wire report was read, and SOURCES.md names
  the wire itself as the confirming outlet.
- When outlets disagreed on a number, the number was left off
  the page. No figure in this edition was disputed between its
  confirming outlets, so nothing had to be dropped on that rule.

## Wires and newspapers, and why both are needed

A wire report and a newspaper report confirm a story in
different ways. The wire's incentive is republication: an error
in a wire story is repeated by every subscriber, so wires check
for their customers' sake. A newspaper's incentive is its own
readers, and its reporting adds the local knowledge a wire's
generalist correspondent may not have. When the two agree, as
AP and The Guardian did on the Iowa numbers, the agreement
crosses both incentive systems, which is stronger than either
alone. When only newspapers agree, the story may still run, but
the paper prefers at least one wire or public broadcaster in
every story's confirmation set, and this edition has one in all
seven.

## Corrections

If a confirming outlet corrects a story after this edition is
printed, the correction belongs to the next edition's record,
not to a silent edit of this one. A submitted entry is a fixed
artefact, like a printed newspaper: it can be wrong in the ways
the day was wrong, and the honest remedy is a correction in the
open, in the following day's SOURCES.md, naming what changed.
No correction was needed for any story in this edition at the
time of submission.
